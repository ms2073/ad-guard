# Ad compliance rules: compounded GLP-1 telehealth ads

Derived from public FDA warning-letter themes (2025–2026) and platform health-ad policies.
This is a demo rule set, not legal advice.

| ID | Rule | Example violation |
|----|------|-------------------|
| R1 | Never call a compounded product "generic" or a generic version of a branded drug. | "Generic Ozempic, delivered" |
| R2 | Never claim the compounded product is the same as, identical to, or equivalent to an FDA-approved drug (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound). Calling it a generic of one counts. Saying it is *not* the same is a correct disclaimer. | "Same medication as Wegovy, half the price" |
| R3 | Never state or imply the compounded product is FDA-approved, FDA-reviewed for safety/efficacy, or clinically proven as compounded. | "FDA-approved weight loss shots" |
| R4 | Never present the drug as the company's own branded product in a way that implies the company makes or compounds it. | "DirectMeds Semaglutide, our formula" |
| R5 | No guaranteed, specific, or exaggerated outcome claims (numbers, timelines, "guaranteed", "melts fat", "effortless"), including testimonials that state results. | "Lose 30 lbs in 30 days, guaranteed" |
| R6 | No doctor, pharmacist or patient endorsements or testimonials, named or unnamed, "verified" or not. They can't be verified at review time, so all of them block. | "Dr. Smith says this is the only shot that works"; "I lost 40 pounds!" - Jessica, verified member |
| R7 | Must say the product is compounded and that a prescription is required and a licensed provider decides eligibility. | Ad omits "compounded" or "prescription" |
| R8 | No minimizing risk ("no side effects", "100% safe", "risk-free"). | "Zero side effects, totally safe" |

Severity: every rule is blocking. R7 is an omission check (must be present), the rest are presence checks.

## In images
The same rules apply to what an image shows, not only to its words:
- Before/after bodies, or a wide figure turning into a slim one, imply an outcome (R5).
- Branded drug packaging or pens shown as the product imply equivalence (R2).
- A person presented as a doctor or patient vouching for the product is an endorsement (R6).
- Small print counts as much as the headline.
